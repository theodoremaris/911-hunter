Step-by-Step: How a Listing Gets Scored

  1. Historical data collection. scrape_historical.py visits every Bring a Trailer sold listing for G-body Porsche 911s and records the final sale price, mileage, color, model variant, and descriptive flags like "matching numbers" or "rust." This builds the training dataset.
  2. Active listing collection. scrape.py hits 6 platforms (BaT, eBay Motors, AutoTrader, Cars.com, Cars & Bids, PCarMarket) and extracts every active G-body 911 for sale in the US. Each listing gets a stable ID based on a hash of its URL.
  3. Feature engineering. Each listing — historical and active — gets converted into 14 numeric features: year, model rank, mileage, transmission type, color premium coefficient, six boolean flags, prior owners, and BaT bid count.
  4. Model training. A Gradient Boosting Regressor (scikit-learn) is trained on historical sale prices using 5-fold cross-validation. GBR was chosen because it handles mixed feature types, non-linear relationships, and small datasets better than linear models.
  5. Scoring. Each active listing is fed into the trained model to produce a predicted_price. The deal_score is calculated as: (predicted − asking) / predicted × 100. A positive score means the car is priced below what the market data suggests.

What Each Feature Means

Mileage

The single strongest predictor. Lower mileage = higher price, with a steep discount above ~120k miles and a significant premium below 50k.

Year / Age

Later production years (1984–1989 Carrera era) command higher prices than early 1970s cars in most model categories. The 3.2L Carrera period is especially sought-after.

Model Variant (Ordinal)

Encoded as a number from 1–8: Turbo (8) → Carrera RS (7) → RS (6) → SC (5) → Carrera (4) → Targa/Cabriolet (3) → 911S (3) → 911E/T (2) → base (1). This reflects real-world collector hierarchy.

Matching Numbers

Means the engine case number stamped at the factory matches the car's VIN records. Cars with mismatched engines trade at a material discount. Matching numbers is one of the most consistently rewarded attributes on BaT.

Manual Transmission

The 4- and 5-speed manual gearboxes are almost universally preferred over the Sportomatic (semi-automatic) by collectors. Manual cars command a consistent premium — the model captures this from the training data.

Rust Flag

Any mention of rust in the listing description is parsed as a negative flag. G-body 911s are particularly susceptible to rust in the battery box, rockers, and floor pans — serious rust can wipe out 20–30% of a car's value.

Project Car Flag

The single largest discount factor. Cars described as "project," "needs work," or "barn find" are priced heavily below finished examples — often 30–50% lower than a comparable driver-quality car.

Color Premium

Rare factory colors (Tangerine, Minerva Blue, Signal Orange, Slate Grey, Viper Green) command documented premiums on BaT. Common colors (silver, white, black) are treated as neutral. Brown and gold are discounted.

Euro / RoW Spec

European-spec and Rest-of-World (RoW) cars often have higher compression engines and lack US safety bumpers, making them desirable to collectors. This typically commands a modest premium when properly documented.

Auction Bid Count (BaT)

Used as a proxy for desirability on historical BaT data only. More bids at auction correlate with higher final prices. This feature is not available for non-BaT listings and defaults to 0.

Limitations & Disclaimers

  • Training data size. The model is only as good as the historical data available. With fewer than ~50 sales in the training set, treat predictions as rough estimates, not appraisals. More BaT history = better predictions.
  • Condition is hard to quantify. Two cars with the same year, model, and mileage can differ by $40k based on condition. The model captures flags like "rust" and "recent service" but cannot assess photos or inspection reports.
  • Market timing. G-body 911 prices have moved significantly over the past 5 years. The model is a snapshot of the market at the time the training data was scraped.
  • Scraper accuracy. Web scrapers are brittle — they break when websites change their HTML. If a scraper returns 0 results, the site may have changed. The scripts log clear warnings when this happens.
  • This is not a professional appraisal. Always inspect a car in person (or hire a PPI — Pre-Purchase Inspection) before buying. This tool is a starting point for research, not a substitute for due diligence.

Data Sources

Bring a Trailer

Primary source for both active listings and historical sold results. BaT auctions tend to attract well-documented, enthusiast-quality cars. Used as the training dataset for the pricing model.

eBay Motors

Largest volume of listings; price range varies widely. Includes both dealer and private party listings. Good for finding off-the-radar deals.

AutoTrader

Primarily dealer inventory. Tends toward higher asking prices. Useful for understanding what dealers think the market will bear.

Cars.com

Mix of dealer and private listings. Similar to AutoTrader in composition.

Cars & Bids

Doug DeMuro's auction site. Focuses on enthusiast cars from the 1980s–2000s. Well-curated listings with good photo and description quality.

PCarMarket

Porsche-specific auction platform. Listings tend to be well-documented and Porsche-enthusiast oriented.
